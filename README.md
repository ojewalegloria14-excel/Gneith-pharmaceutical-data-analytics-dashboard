# Gneith pharmaceutical-data-analytics-dashboard

**Project Overview**

GNEITH Pharmaceutical Industry is a fictional pharmaceutical company created for this data analytics project.

The goal of this project was to use sales, inventory, and production data to understand what is happening in the business, where problems may exist, and where the company can improve.

I worked with raw and cleaned data, used SQL to clean and analyze the data, and created Power BI dashboards to turn the results into clear business insights.


**This project uses data to answer questions such as:** 

1. Which products are driving sales and revenue? 

2. Which products need inventory attention? 

3. How efficiently is production performing?


**What I Did**

1. I Cleaned and prepared data using Excel, SQL, and Power BI
2. I Analyzed business data using SQL
3. I Created KPIs and interactive dashboards using Power BI and DAX
4. I Identified trends and potential business issues

**Key Insights**

1. Sales are generating revenue, but the downward trend needs attention.

GNEITH generated 2.37M in revenue and 578.37K in profit from approximately 258K units sold. However, monthly revenue showed a general decline over the period. This means the company should find out what is causing sales to fall before it affects future revenue.

2. Some medicines may not be available when customers need them.

There are 293 low-stock records and 20 out-of-stock records. If these products are in high demand, customers may not be able to get them, which could lead to missed sales and unhappy customers.

3. A large amount of money is tied up in inventory.

The company has approximately 196.76M worth of inventory and around 3M units in stock. Some products are also close to expiry. This means GNEITH needs to make sure it is holding the right products in the right quantities instead of tying up money in slow-moving stock.

4. Production is performing well, but product quality should still be monitored.

GNEITH produced about 13M units, with a strong 98.62% yield and a low 1.38% rejection rate. However, Salbutamol had the highest rejection rate, so its production process may need closer investigation.



**Business Recommendations**
1. Investigate the falling sales trend.

Look closely at customer demand, product performance, regions, and sales channels to understand why monthly revenue is declining and where improvements are needed.

2. Improve stock planning for important medicines.

Prioritize the 293 low-stock and 20 out-of-stock records, especially for products with strong demand. This can help reduce stockouts and protect sales.

3. Match inventory and production with actual demand.

Use sales data to guide how much of each medicine should be produced and stored. This can help prevent excess inventory, reduce the risk of expiry, and avoid tying up too much money in stock.

4. Investigate Salbutamol's high rejection rate.

Review the production process, materials, equipment, and quality checks to understand why more Salbutamol units are being rejected.

**Business Impact**

If GNEITH acts on these findings, the company could:

 1. Reduce stockouts and protect sales.
    
 2. Reduce money tied up in unnecessary or slow-moving inventory.
    
 3. Lower the risk of waste from products approaching expiry.
    
 4. Reduce production waste and improve product quality.
